# Example: Pre-Analysis in practice

This walkthrough shows how to use [`bardin-pre-analysis`](../../en/content-analysis-bardin/bardin-pre-analysis/SKILL.md) on a small corpus.

## The request

> Using the `bardin-pre-analysis` skill, build the corpus protocol for the three documents below. My research question is: how do teachers describe the use of digital tools in the classroom?

## The input (abridged)

- **D1:** Interview with a public-school teacher (transcript, 12 pages).
- **D2:** Interview with a private-school teacher (transcript, 9 pages).
- **D3:** School pedagogical project (institutional document, 20 pages).

## What the skill produces

### 1. Floating reading matrix

| Doc ID | Highlighted excerpt | Initial impression | Relevant question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| D1 | "I only use the projector when the internet works." | Infrastructure as a barrier | How often does infrastructure appear? |
| D2 | "The platform saves me planning time." | Efficiency framing | Which tools are named as time-savers? |
| D3 | "Digital resources are encouraged but not mandatory." | Institutional ambiguity | Is the policy binding or advisory? |

### 2. Corpus audit (Bardin's 4 rules)

| Rule | Status | Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Exhaustiveness | Met | All three collected documents are included. |
| Representativeness | Partially met | Two teachers only; acknowledge as a limitation. |
| Homogeneity | Met for D1/D2, adapted for D3 | Interviews vs. institutional document are analyzed separately. |
| Relevance | Met | All documents address digital tools in the classroom. |

### 3. Objectives, hypotheses, and indices

- **Objective:** describe how teachers frame digital tools.
- **Reading hypothesis:** infrastructure is described as the main obstacle.
- **Indices:** tool names, barrier words ("internet", "equipment"), and evaluative adjectives ("saves", "waste").

### 4. Corpus protocol (excerpt)

> Corpus composed of two teacher interviews (D1, D2) and one institutional document (D3). Interviews were coded together; D3 was used as contextual policy data. Representativeness is limited to two cases and will be reported as a limitation.

## Why this helps

The output is not a finished analysis. It is an auditable record of *why* the corpus was chosen, which is exactly what an examination board asks about later.
