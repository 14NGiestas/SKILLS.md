# Bardin: Categorization and Traceability Matrix

> **English** | [Português](../../../pt-br/analise-de-conteudo-bardin/bardin-categorizacao/SKILL.md)

Skill to operationalize Step 5 (Categorization) of Phase 2 (Exploration of Material) in Laurence Bardin's Content Analysis (2011/2016). Organizes Recording Units (RU) into robust and auditable analytical categories.

## When to Use

- Following the coding stage and extraction of Recording Units.
- To structure *a priori* (theory-driven), *a posteriori* (data-driven), or mixed categories.
- To test the methodological validity of the categorical system against Bardin's 5 formal criteria.
- To construct the category dictionary and full traceability matrix for academic defense.

## Steps of Categorization

### 1. Selecting the Categorical Model
- **A Priori (Deductive):** Applies pre-existing categories originating from the theoretical framework.
- **A Posteriori (Inductive):** Allows categories to emerge from the analogical grouping of raw data.
- **Mixed:** Employs broad theoretical categories while allowing emergent subcategories to arise from data.

### 2. Analogical Grouping of Recording Units
- Gather RUs by thematic and semantic affinity.
- Establish hierarchical structure: `Master Category` -> `Subcategory` -> `Codes / RUs`.

### 3. Auditing Bardin's 5 Criteria
Verify and confirm that the categorical system strictly satisfies each criterion:
1. **Mutual Exclusion:** Each RU belongs to one and only one category (no ambiguous overlap).
2. **Homogeneity:** A single classification principle governs the entire category (elements of the same nature).
3. **Relevance:** Categories directly answer the research problem, objectives, and hypotheses.
4. **Objectivity and Fidelity:** Definitions are sufficiently clear so that different coders reach identical results.
5. **Productivity:** Categories are fertile, generating new insights and relevant findings.

### 4. Constructing the Category Dictionary
For each created category and subcategory, define:
- **Category Name**
- **Conceptual Definition**
- **Inclusion Rule:** What qualifies a passage for inclusion in the category.
- **Exclusion Rule:** What prevents a passage from entering the category.
- **Typical Example:** Representative textual evidence.

### 5. Generating the Categorical Traceability Matrix
Structure the final traceability document connecting: `Master Category` | `Subcategory` | `RU (Code)` | `Textual Evidence (Citation)` | `Doc ID / Participant`

## Gotchas

- **Overlapping Categories:** If an RU fits into two categories simultaneously, revise the category dictionary to ensure mutual exclusion.
- **"Junk Drawer" Categories:** Avoid creating generic categories (e.g., "Other" or "Miscellaneous") without well-defined analytical criteria.
- **Descriptive Rather Than Analytical Names:** Prefer category names that reveal meaning or participant attitude rather than mere broad topics.
