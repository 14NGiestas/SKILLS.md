# Bardin: Inference, Interpretation, and Methodological Defense

Skill to operationalize Phase 3 (Treatment of Results, Inference, and Interpretation) of Laurence Bardin's Content Analysis methodology (2011/2016). Connects empirical categories to the theoretical framework and prepares the researcher to defend the validity of the analysis before an examination board.

## When to Use

- Following consolidation of the Categorical Traceability Matrix.
- To transition from manifest content (explicitly stated data) to latent content (underlying meanings, silences, ideologies, and contradictions).
- To articulate empirical findings with authors from the theoretical framework.
- To draft the Methodology and Results/Discussion chapters.
- To simulate examination board questioning regarding category construction and data validity.

## Steps of Inference and Interpretation

### 1. Manifest and Latent Content Analysis
- **Manifest Content:** Summarize what data explicitly exposes and measures (frequencies, predominant themes, and recurring patterns).
- **Latent Content:** Apply Bardin's detective metaphor to infer what is unsaid:
  - What does silence or absence regarding a subject indicate?
  - What internal contradictions or ambiguities arise in participants' discourse?
  - What stereotypes, implicit values, or underlying ideologies sustain statements?

### 2. Theoretical Triangulation and Discussion of Findings
- Cross-reference each analytical category with concepts and authors defined in the research's theoretical framework.
- Identify whether empirical findings confirm, challenge, extend, or reshape propositions in existing literature.
- Directly and rigorously address the research questions and objectives established in Pre-Analysis.

### 3. Drafting the Results and Discussion Chapter
- Structure data presentation by combining:
  1. Conceptual introduction of the category and subcategory.
  2. Presentation of matrix/table containing evidence and frequencies.
  3. Illustrative textual excerpt (RU/CU).
  4. Analytical discussion and interpretation light of theory.

### 4. Examination Board Simulation and Methodological Defense
- Prepare grounded answers to common examination board questions:
  - "How did you arrive at these categories?" -> Answer by demonstrating the auditable trajectory: floating reading, RUs, rules of mutual exclusion, and homogeneity.
  - "Isn't this analysis purely subjective?" -> Highlight formal safeguards (the 4 corpus rules and 5 categorization criteria) that guarantee controlled hermeneutics.

## Gotchas

- **Mere Paraphrasing of Data:** Do not limit yourself to repeating in different words what participant quotes already state. Inference requires going beyond text.
- **Isolation from Theory:** Avoid presenting categorization separated from the theoretical framework. Every category must engage with literature.
- **Lack of Traceability in Writing:** Whenever quoting a passage, maintain anonymous participant identifiers and corpus location.
