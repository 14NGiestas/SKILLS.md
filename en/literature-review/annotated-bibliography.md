# Academic Reading Notes & Annotated Bibliography

Skill for operationalizing systematic reading notes and annotated bibliographies of scientific literature (articles, books, theses, and dissertations) in accordance with scientific methodology standards.

## When to Use

- To summarize articles and book chapters read in undergraduate or graduate courses.
- To organize literature reviews for bachelor's theses, master's dissertations, and doctoral dissertations.
- To extract verbatim quotes with exact page numbering for future use in academic citations (APA/Chicago/MLA).

## Required Structure

### 1. Bibliographical Identification
- Full reference according to standard guidelines (e.g., APA 7th or Chicago Manual of Style).
- Keywords (3 to 5 terms).
- Document Type (Journal Article, Book Chapter, Thesis, Full Book, etc.).

### 2. Central Thesis and General Structure
- **Research Problem / Core Question:** What the text investigates.
- **Central Thesis / Hypothesis:** The main argument defended by the author in 1 or 2 sentences.
- **Objectives and Methodology:** Purpose of the study and approaches/methods employed.

### 3. Thematic Notes (Summary of Arguments)
- **Development by Sections/Chapters:** Schematic summary of the main points argued throughout the text.
- **Key Concepts:** Definitions of central theoretical terms presented by the author.

### 4. Direct Excerpts (Verbatim Quotes)
- Literal transcription of fundamental passages within quotation marks.
- Indication of exact page numbering at the end of each passage: `"Quote text..." (p. XX)`.
- *Golden Rule:* Do not alter words, punctuation, or structure of the original text inside quotation marks. If page numbers are unavailable in the source document, use `(p. n/a)`.

### 5. Analytical Notes / Critical Appraisal
- **Critical Assessment:** Evaluation of the relevance, clarity, and contribution of the text to the field of study.
- **Theoretical Connection:** How this text relates to other authors in the field or to the researcher's overall project.

## Execution Rules

1. **Separation of Voices:** Maintain a clear distinction between statements made by the author of the text and comments made by the student/researcher.
2. **Page Accuracy:** Do not fabricate page numbers. Rigorously map them based on the provided file/text.
3. **Synthetic Clarity:** Avoid long paragraphs in the thematic summary; favor structured bullet points and concise language.
